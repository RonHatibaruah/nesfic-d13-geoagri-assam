1. PROBLEM UNDERSTANDING & USER WORKFLOW
[Word Count: 329 / 500 words]
================================================================================

Problem Background and Core Gaps:
Assam encompasses over 78,438 sq km across the Brahmaputra and Barak river basins, where agriculture sustains over 70 percent of the rural workforce. Despite fertile alluvial soil, vast tracts of arable land—including seasonal culturable fallows, riverine char-chapori silt edges, underutilized tea garden grant lands, and post-monsoon flood-recessed plains—remain uncultivated, keeping state cropping intensity at around 155 percent.

State agricultural planning faces three critical bottlenecks:
1. Siloed and Coarse Data: Land classification relies on legacy revenue books (Chitha and Jamabandi) and coarse decadal maps that fail to capture micro-seasonal fallow dynamics.
2. Dynamic Agro-Hydrological Risk: The Brahmaputra deposits millions of tons of fertile silt annually. Without multi-spectral remote sensing, field officers cannot differentiate between permanently waterlogged swamps and high-fertility silt deposits primed for immediate post-monsoon Rabi cultivation.
3. Absence of Unified Spatial Support: District Agriculture Officers (DAOs) and field officers lack an integrated spatial map linking soil health (pH, NPK, organic carbon), water distance, slope, and cadastral parcel boundaries.

Target Beneficiaries:
- Primary: Department of Agriculture and Horticulture, Assam State Space Application Centre (ASSAC), District Agricultural Offices, and Farmer Producer Organizations (FPOs).
- Secondary: Smallholder and marginal farmers, rural youth agri-entrepreneurs, and revenue departments implementing state and central schemes such as MOVCDNER, PM-KISAN, and Chief Minister Samagra Gramya Unnayan Yojana (CMSGUY).

Current versus Intended Workflow:
- Current Workflow: Village-level paper survey leading to Manual compilation at block office leading to Subjective ground inspection with high human error and 6 to 8 week delays leading to Delayed scheme sanctioning and widespread fallow neglect.
- Intended Outcome: An automated, cloud-based Geospatial Decision Support System (DSS) named "GeoAgri-Assam" that ingests multi-temporal Sentinel-2 (optical) and Sentinel-1 (C-band SAR) imagery, Cartosat digital elevation models, and digitized Soil Health Card grids. It automatically detects, classifies, and ranks vacant and fallow lands down to cadastral parcel resolution, generating a Fertility and Cultivability Index (FCI) to guide precision crop allocation, irrigation investments, and farmer lease allocations with Greater than 88 percent spatial accuracy.

2. PROPOSED SOLUTION & KEY FEATURES
[Word Count: 340 / 500 words]
================================================================================

Solution Overview:
"GeoAgri-Assam" is a cloud-native geospatial intelligence platform calibrated for Assam's agro-ecological zones. It seamlessly fuses multi-spectral satellite remote sensing, Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR), hydrological modeling, and digital soil health registers to automate the detection, classification, and suitability scoring of cultivable, underutilized, and fertile land parcels.

Main Modules and Functionality:
1. Automated Fallow and Wasteland Detection Engine: Ingests bi-weekly Sentinel-2 (10m) and Landsat-9 imagery. Computes phenological time-series indices—NDVI (vegetation), NDWI (moisture), and Bare Soil Index (BSI)—to isolate seasonal fallows (Kharif, Rabi, Zaid) from permanent barren wastelands and dense forests.
2. SAR Hydrological and Flood Masking Subsystem: Employs Sentinel-1 Dual-Pol SAR data to map historical flood inundation footprints and drainage congestion. It separates high-risk flood zones from flood-receded, silt-enriched fertile tracts suitable for short-duration pulses, mustard, and Boro rice.
3. Composite Fertility and Cultivability Index (FCI): Calculates parcel-level agricultural potential using a Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (AHP) algorithm fusing:
- Biophysical Fertility: Soil Organic Carbon (SOC), NPK distribution, and Soil pH digitized from state Soil Health Card databases.
- Topographic Suitability: Slope, aspect, and elevation derived from Cartosat 30m DEMs.
- Irrigation and Infrastructure: Distance to Brahmaputra river channels, canals, roads, and power infrastructure.
4. AI Crop Suitability Matcher: Applies an XGBoost recommendation model that maps identified cultivable parcels to high-value North-Eastern crops suited for regional soils (Joha and black rice, Assam lemon / Kaji Nemu, ginger, turmeric, and TS-36/38 mustard).
5. Interactive Web GIS Dashboard and Mobile Ground-Truthing PWA: A role-based web GIS interface for state and district officials to filter lands by district, circle, parcel size, and fertility rating, paired with an offline mobile Progressive Web App for field verification by local Agricultural Development Officers (ADOs).

Key Changes versus Current Approach:
- Shifts state agricultural planning from subjective annual manual surveys to automated satellite analytics refreshed every 10 to 12 days.
- Unifies disparate data (revenue cadastre, soil cards, satellite feeds, flood recurrence) into a single map layer.
- Enables targeted interventions: converting fallow tracts into commercial production clusters managed by FPOs with quantifiable acreage and estimated yield returns.

4. TECHNOLOGY & SOLUTION ARCHITECTURE

Technology Stack:
- Frontend and GIS Visualization: React 18, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, Leaflet and MapLibre GL for GPU-accelerated raster and vector tile streaming, Lucide icons, and responsive charting.
- Backend and Spatial Microservices: Python FastAPI (Python 3.11+) and Node.js for high-throughput spatial querying; GDAL, Rasterio, Shapely, and GeoPandas for spatial vector-raster clipping and zonal statistics.
- Spatial Database: PostgreSQL 16 with PostGIS extension for spatial querying (R-tree spatial indexing, ST_Intersection, ST_DWithin); Redis for caching multi-resolution map tiles and query filters.
- Earth Observation Data Sources: ESA Copernicus Sentinel-2 MSI (10m optical) and Sentinel-1 C-Band SAR; Cartosat and SRTM 30m Digital Elevation Models (DEM); Digital Soil Health Card registers.
- Machine Learning Models: Random Forest and U-Net classifiers for harmonic phenological fallow detection; Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP) for composite fertility scoring; XGBoost classifier for crop suitability matching.
- Hosting and Infrastructure: Cloudflare Pages global Edge CDN for the web application, with Docker containerized backend services deployable on Assam State Data Centre (SDC) or NIC MeghRaj Cloud.

Solution Architecture and Data Flow:
1. Ingestion Tier: Automated scheduled workers pull Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 tiles via Copernicus Open Access APIs and ingest village-level Soil Health Card data.
2. Preprocessing Tier: Atmospheric correction and cloud masking performed via Sen2Cor algorithms; computation of multi-temporal spectral indices (NDVI, NDWI, Bare Soil Index) and SAR backscatter intensities.
3. Spatial Analytics Tier: AI fallow classification models disentangle single-cropped fallows from double-cropped lands; the AHP weighting engine calculates the Fertility and Cultivability Index (FCI) on a 0 to 100 scale.
4. Database and API Tier: PostGIS database stores vectorized parcels with spatial R-tree indices; FastAPI exposes REST, WMS, and GeoJSON endpoints with sub-second response times.
5. Presentation Tier: Departmental web GIS portal and mobile Progressive Web App (PWA) provide real-time spatial filtering, parcel dossier inspection, and one-click GeoJSON export.

3. Sparse Soil Health Data: Incomplete physical soil test records in remote rural blocks. Mitigation: Employ spatial Kriging and machine learning transfer learning to interpolate soil fertility proxies, validated against KVK benchmark records.

- Dual-District Pilot (Months 4 to 5): Deploy the pilot across two contrasting agro-ecological districts: Kamrup Rural (high alluvial plain with extensive seasonal fallows) and Barpeta (riparian char basin with post-monsoon silt tracts).

1. Greater than or equal to 88 percent accuracy in delineating cultivable fallow parcels.
